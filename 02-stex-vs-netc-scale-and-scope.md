# 02 — STEX vs NETC/InnoMatch: Scale, Scope and What "Largest" Means

> **About this document**
>
> - **Purpose:** Compare STEX and NETC/InnoMatch on transaction scale, ecosystem breadth and platform revenue, and explain how each turns a technology into a deal.
> - **Audience:** Founders, product managers and analysts. Chinese terms (挂牌 listing, 竞价 competitive bidding, 进场 on-exchange, 亿 = 100 million) are defined in [01 — Key terms](01-shanghai-landscape.md#key-terms).
> - **Status:** Market research, corrected against primary sources (see "Corrections" at the end).
> - **Previous:** [01 — Shanghai landscape](01-shanghai-landscape.md) · **Next:** [03 — Business models and revenue](03-business-models-and-revenue.md)

---

## Summary

"Which of the two platforms is bigger?" has three different answers, because three different things can be measured:

| Question | Answer | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Larger **technology marketplace by transaction value** | **STEX** | ¥30.9B on-exchange in 2024, against ≈ ¥4.2B of signed deals facilitated by NETC; > ¥63.4B cumulative |
| Wider **technology-transfer ecosystem** (variety, demand side, services, international network) | **NETC + InnoMatch** | Demand-driven matching, technology managers, international network, events, training |
| Larger **platform business by revenue** (2023) | **NETC's operating company** (¥46.2M) vs **STEX** (¥28.4M) | Fund-prospectus financial disclosures |

> **Key rule:** transaction value ≠ platform revenue. STEX's 2023 revenue was about **0.15%** of the deal value it handled.

## 1. Qualitative comparison

| Dimension | STEX (上海技术交易所) | NETC / InnoMatch (国家技术转移东部中心) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core model | Technology **exchange** (licensed trading venue) | Technology-transfer **ecosystem + matching platform** |
| Transaction infrastructure | **Strong:** registration, disclosure, auction/tender/bidding, third-party settlement, transaction certificates | Matching, brokerage and service oriented |
| Publicly reported transaction value | **Higher and well documented** | Large, but reported as "facilitated" or "intended" value |
| Breadth of online content | Large | **Very broad** (demands, results, solutions, talent) |
| Corporate demand side | Strong | **Exceptional:** demand collection is its core |
| Universities and research institutes | Very strong (pricing and disclosure services for public institutions) | Very strong (results sourcing) |
| Professional services | Coordinates service providers on the venue | **Broader:** brokers, training, evaluation, incubation, finance |
| International reach | Growing (Lingang international center, 32 cooperating countries) | **Very strong** (global network, InnoMatch EXPO) |
| Best one-line description | "Technology marketplace / exchange" | "Technology-transfer super-platform / ecosystem" |

These ratings are analytical judgments based on public information, not measured scores.

## 2. Largest marketplace by transaction value: STEX

### 2.1 Verified figures

| Year | Annual transaction value | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2021 | ¥3.047B | Closed value (成交金额), fund prospectus. First full year after the October 2020 re-launch |
| 2022 | ¥10.115B | Closed value, fund prospectus |
| 2023 | ¥18.922B | Closed value, fund prospectus. Compound annual growth 2021–2023: 149.2% |
| 2024 | ¥30.933B on-exchange value (进场交易额); 2,383 technologies entered | STCSM 2024 report |
| Cumulative | > ¥42.99B and 10,435 technologies (26 Jul 2024) → > ¥63.4B and > 12,800 technologies (end of 2024) → > ¥65.3B and > 13,000 technologies (June 2025, STCSM) → ≈ ¥76B and > 13,000 technologies (July 2025, STEX president) | Different dates and definitions; see [01 §1.4](01-shanghai-landscape.md) |

> **Note on definitions:** sources use different measures: *on-exchange transaction value* (进场交易额), *closed transaction value* (成交金额) and *cumulative* values at different cut-off dates. The 2021–2023 and 2024 annual figures therefore come from two different metrics, although their sum (≈ ¥63.0B) is consistent with the > ¥63.4B cumulative closed value at the end of 2024. Always quote the metric name and date with the number.

### 2.2 Why STEX is the "exchange" benchmark

STEX performs the functions that make a market an *exchange* rather than a directory: rights registration, tiered disclosure, price discovery through competitive methods, third-party settlement and transaction certification (see [01 §1.2–1.3](01-shanghai-landscape.md)).

## 3. Widest ecosystem: NETC + InnoMatch

InnoMatch is not just a database of technologies. It connects every participant needed to commercialize a technology:

```mermaid
flowchart TB
    IM(("InnoMatch"))
    T["Technology results<br/>(universities, institutes, companies)"] --- IM
    D["Corporate technology demands"] --- IM
    R["Researchers and R&D teams"] --- IM
    TM["Technology managers / brokers<br/>(技术经理人)"] --- IM
    C["Capital<br/>(investors, technology finance)"] --- IM
    S["Professional services<br/>(evaluation, IP, legal)"] --- IM
    TL["Talent"] --- IM
    IM ==> COM["Commercialization<br/>pilots, transactions, new ventures"]
```

Official figures: NETC facilitated **≈ ¥4.2 billion** of signed deals in 2024 and **≈ ¥40 billion** cumulatively (STCSM 2024 report). The same report gives InnoMatch platform totals, **cumulative from the August 2022 launch to the end of 2024** (also published on NETC's website; more dates in [01 §2.3](01-shanghai-landscape.md)):

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Corporate demands | 6,468 |
| Technology results | 4,390 |
| Solutions received | 3,015 |
| Completed transactions | 313 |
| Completed ("landed") value | ¥778M |
| Matching intentions in 2024 alone | 595, with > ¥3.4B intended signing value |

The September 2025 InnoMatch fair released **more than 10,000 technology needs**, plus **1,728 R&D results** seeking commercialization and more than ¥20B of investment commitments from participating companies.

For **variety, discovery, demand-side depth and matching**, NETC/InnoMatch is ahead of STEX.

## 4. Platform revenue: now measurable

An earlier draft said neither platform's revenue could be found. In fact, both operating companies' financials appear in a public fund prospectus. The fund is a listed infrastructure REIT (real-estate investment trust) that owns incubator buildings in Yangpu; STEX and the NETC operating company are tenants of those buildings, so the prospectus discloses their finances as cash-flow providers.

| ¥ million | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | H1 2024 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **STEX** (上海技术交易所有限公司), revenue | 23.17 | 28.40 | **28.43** | 9.57 |
| STEX, net profit | −13.75 | −11.87 | **6.25** | −19.86 |
| **NETC operating company** (上海东部科技成果转化有限公司), revenue | 31.85 | 35.05 | **46.20** | 3.84 |
| NETC operating company, net profit | 2.66 | 1.72 | **1.75** | −24.23 |

Observations:

1. **Both are small businesses compared with the deal value they handle.** STEX's 2023 revenue was ≈ 0.15% of its ¥18.9B transaction value.
2. **NETC's operating company earned more revenue than STEX in 2023**, despite reporting far less *completed* transaction value. This is consistent with a diversified service model: consulting, training, events, government programme operation and incubation.
3. **Income arrives late in the year.** STEX recognizes most of its revenue in the second half, and its government subsidies (booked as "other income", not revenue) usually arrive in the second half too. NETC's operating company recognizes most of its revenue in the fourth quarter. This explains the first-half losses in 2024.
4. **Government support matters.** STEX attributes swings in profit partly to the timing of government subsidies and of gains from selling equity investments.

> **Caveat:** the NETC operating company is not the whole NETC network. Network partners and affiliated entities also earn revenue, so this figure is a floor for the NETC model, not a total.

## 5. How each platform turns a technology into a deal

### 5.1 STEX: listing-based exchange flow (typical)

This is the general sequence in STEX's published rules for a formal listing (挂牌), with the time limits from [09 §2.3](09-reference-platform-features.md). Product-specific rules vary. Rights registration (技术权益登记) is a separate STEX service that can precede a listing; it is not a mandatory step.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A["Owner applies to list a technology<br/>(rights evidence, internal approvals)"] --> B["Exchange checks completeness and reviews<br/>ownership, authority and compliance (≤ 3 WD)"]
    B --> D["Listing announcement and public disclosure<br/>(挂牌公告, ≥ 5 WD)"]
    D --> E["Intended buyers register through a practising agent<br/>(登记意向受让方) and are qualified"]
    E --> F{"How many qualified<br/>buyers?"}
    F -->|"One"| G["Agreement at or above the floor price<br/>(协议转让)"]
    F -->|"Two or more"| H["Competitive method announced in advance:<br/>online bidding, auction, tender or negotiation"]
    F -->|"None"| X["Extend disclosure (≥ 5 WD) or terminate"]
    G --> I["Contract signed within 5 WD,<br/>reviewed by the exchange"]
    H --> I
    I --> J["Third-party settlement of price and deposits<br/>(bank or licensed payment institution)"]
    J --> K["Transaction certificate issued<br/>(交易凭证 / 交易鉴证)"]
    K --> L["Post-deal: technology-contract registration,<br/>patent recordal, benefit distribution"]
```

WD = working days.

**Where our platform fits.** As a registered practising agent, our platform prepares and checks the seller's application, registers buyers on behalf of clients (the agent bears joint liability for buyer materials, STEX rule 82), tracks every deadline and stores the venue's certificate numbers. The venue performs the review, disclosure, competitive sale, settlement and certification ([10 §3.10](10-product-requirements.md)).

### 5.2 NETC/InnoMatch: demand-driven matching flow (typical)

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A["Company states a technology need"] --> B["Technology manager clarifies and<br/>structures the demand"]
    B --> C["Need published online and pushed<br/>to the domestic and overseas network"]
    C --> D["Universities, institutes and companies<br/>submit results or solutions"]
    D --> E["Online matching plus offline<br/>screening by technology managers"]
    E --> F["Roadshows, matchmaking meetings,<br/>InnoMatch EXPO sessions"]
    F --> G["Evaluation, pilot or proof of concept"]
    G --> H{"Deal route"}
    H -->|"License / transfer"| I["Transaction, optionally<br/>on a licensed exchange"]
    H -->|"Joint development"| J["R&D contract"]
    H -->|"New venture"| K["Incubation and financing"]
```

### 5.3 The difference in one line

| | STEX | InnoMatch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chain | evaluation and floor price → listing and disclosure → buyer registration → negotiation or competition → contract → settlement → certificate | demand → structuring → network sourcing → matching → expert/broker service → pilot → commercialization |
| Starts from | A **technology** someone wants to sell | A **problem** someone wants solved |
| Main value | Trust, price discovery and legal certainty | Discovery, matching and service |

## 6. Implications for our platform

1. **Benchmark both, but build only one.** InnoMatch is the model for the *demand and network* layer, which we build ourselves. STEX is the model for the *transaction* layer, which we reach as a registered practising agent of a licensed venue (STEX or CTEX) instead of rebuilding it.
2. **Do not measure success by transaction value alone.** Track platform-earned revenue, completed transfers and post-deal outcomes separately (see [05 §19](05-system-design-blueprint.md)).
3. **Expect a service-heavy revenue mix.** Both Shanghai operators earn modest revenue relative to deal value, and the network operator earns more through services.
4. **Combining the two models is the opportunity.** Shanghai runs them as two companies under one group. Our product can connect them more tightly: one record carries a deal from demand and matching through to the venue's listing, settlement and certificate (see [04](04-product-strategy.md) and [10 §1.1](10-product-requirements.md)).
