Yes. If **your goal is to build a technology exchange + technology-transfer platform**, I would **combine STEX + NETC/InnoMatch**, but I would _not_ simply clone either one.

The strongest model is:

> **NETC's supply/demand network + STEX's trusted transaction infrastructure + a modern SaaS/marketplace experience + AI-assisted technology matching and evaluation.**

The key insight is that **technology transfer is not primarily a listing problem**. It is a **trust + discovery + evaluation + matching + transaction + commercialization problem**.

Shanghai's own 2025 rules are very revealing: a technology exchange is expected to support rights registration, transaction certification, information disclosure, market-oriented evaluation, settlement, professional service providers, risk control, and dispute resolution—not merely publish listings. [Shanghai Municipal Government](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/nw12344/20250414/7d1e61e9a2de431aa524cb2f817845cf.html?siteId=1&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 3. But I would make one major change

I would **not start with a giant technology database**.

That's one of the biggest traps.

Imagine launching:

> "We have 100,000 technologies!"

That sounds impressive.

But a company arrives and says:

> "I need a battery thermal-management technology that can survive 150°C, has TRL 7+, is manufacturable below $X/unit, and can be licensed in Europe."

A database doesn't solve that.

Your platform should instead ask:

Then turn that into a structured demand.

## My strongest recommendation

If you want to build a **serious international technology-transfer company**, I would use:

**STEX as the model for the "Exchange" layer**

- **NETC/InnoMatch as the model for the "Transfer ecosystem" layer**

- **LinkedIn + Bloomberg + Alibaba-style UX as inspiration for the modern digital layer**

- **AI as the intelligence layer**

The result is something neither STEX nor NETC fully represents today:

> **A global technology marketplace where technologies are searchable like securities/products, evaluated like investments, matched like jobs, negotiated like M&A, and transferred through a controlled transaction workflow.**

That is the direction I would pursue.

And there is one particularly important thing I'd do **before writing any code**: map the complete **STEX + NETC/InnoMatch user journeys and revenue flows screen-by-screen**, identify what they do well/poorly, and then turn that into a **PRD + database/domain model + marketplace architecture** for your own platform. That would give you a much more concrete foundation than simply copying their feature lists.
